\title{Dual Writes and CDC Replay:\\
  A Mechanized Crash-Safety Characterization}
\author{Andreas Andreakis}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  This is the \emph{Dual Writes Complete} artifact, the machine-checked
  Isabelle/HOL development behind a crash-safety characterization for dual
  writes. The contained implementation session is named
  \isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Core} for build
  compatibility.

  A dual write sends each update to two independent, non-atomic stores. This
  entry studies the change-data-capture (CDC) setting --- the source database's
  committed log is the authority, and a second downstream store is written
  separately --- and turns the practitioner warning against dual writes into a
  \emph{two-sided characterization} of cross-store crash-safety, then shows
  observable crash-safety \emph{invariant} in everything about the delivered
  stream but its scoped image. The development is self-contained over a
  shared DBLog-free
  \isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Layer0} parent (source
  coordinates, histories, scope restriction, replay, and the shared
  virtual-cut-state predicate), so the source-coordinate order is \emph{proved}
  rather than assumed.

  The characterization has four parts. \emph{Obstruction:} for a crash-closed,
  refining implementation, reaching a durable divergent crash is equivalent to
  reaching an \emph{exposable} scoped mismatch --- a total partition of the
  implementation domain, not a one-sided impossibility --- and an effective
  non-atomic source write on a monitored, in-interval key already reaches the
  hazard. \emph{Construction:} the safe side is inhabited by a verified
  non-atomic bounded-replay CDC relay whose single advertise discipline both
  forces a bad crash at an unadvertised frontier and discharges no-mismatch as an
  inductive invariant at every advertised one, with recovery healing by
  non-atomic replay. \emph{Duality:} restricting to running frontiers, a
  crash-closed, refining, running-start implementation is crash-safe if and only
  if its downstream image equals the committed source-log image on scope at every
  reachable running frontier; all three premises are load-bearing, each
  counterexample-witnessed (refinement in both directions).
  \emph{Completeness:} crash-safety depends only on the scoped store image ---
  neither the acknowledgement/durability axis nor the delivery-order axis of the
  delivered stream carries a stronger crash-safety notion. A certified
  \emph{executable} decider settles a concrete schedule's bad-crash status at a
  given frontier and key by kernel evaluation.

  Supporting layers round out the development without extending the contribution
  surface: the absence-shaped
  \isa{asymmetric{\isacharunderscore}store} safety kernel and the operational
  separable non-atomic completion theorem (the specializations the obstruction
  generalizes); an AFP-facing valid-execution wrapper
  (\isa{admissible{\isacharunderscore}dw{\isacharunderscore}exec{\isacharunderscore}trace});
  the DBLog-free replay/CDC positive side (outbox/CDC segments, replay prefixes,
  certified CDC tails); a pairwise fan-out lift over indexed downstream targets;
  a bounded multi-key source-group lift; and delivery, crash-truncation, and
  store-clock refinements. DBLog appears only as an optional companion witness,
  checked in the separate session
  \isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}DBLog{\isacharunderscore}Instance}
  by citation to the
  \isa{DBLog{\isacharunderscore}Virtual{\isacharunderscore}Cuts}
  development~\cite{andreakis2026artifact}; it is not imported or re-derived
  inside \isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Core}.

  Soundness throughout is scoped-state equality at the chosen frontier: per-key
  in the core theorems, and bounded source-group invariant violation in the
  multi-key layer. It is not a claim of general transactional atomicity,
  serializability, isolation, or multidatabase atomic commitment, and the
  obstruction is a crash-durability fact rather than an FLP-style
  asynchronous-consensus result. Every theorem is conditional on the premises
  stated in its statement: the development does not claim that all systems called
  dual writes fail, nor that production CDC runtimes, exactly-once delivery,
  brokers, or retries are verified here. The development is sorry-free and
  axiom-free, the elevated headline theorems are certified oracle-free, and the
  main classes are inhabited by closed-form witnesses, so no result is vacuously
  true.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{The setting}

The original DBLog change-data-capture framework~\cite{andreakis2020dblog}
and its theory~\cite{andreakis2026theoretical} treat the source database's
committed change log as the single authority and reconstruct a
replay-equivalent snapshot of a live scope from it. This entry asks what
happens when, in addition, application code writes the same update to a
\emph{second} store --- the dual-write anti-pattern. The committed log
(\isa{log{\isacharunderscore}image}) remains the authority; the downstream
\isa{store{\isadigit{2}}} is written independently and non-atomically. State
is measured through source-coordinate frontiers. For the downstream, a frontier
is an applied or advertised source offset; the store-clock layer additionally
allows an independent downstream-local clock carrier linked to such offsets by a
faithful acknowledgement relation. A bad crash prefix may catch either durable
side ahead of the other: the mechanized witnesses cover the old source-first
shape, a downstream-first stale-update shape, and a source-first cross-axis
offset-lag shape.

The shared parent theories
(\isa{Source{\isacharunderscore}Coordinates}, \isa{Source{\isacharunderscore}History},
\isa{Scope}, \isa{Replay}, and
\isa{Virtual{\isacharunderscore}Cut{\isacharunderscore}State}) are the DBLog-free
Layer-0 substrate --- source coordinates, histories, scope restriction, replay,
and the shared virtual-cut-state predicate --- on which the dual-write content
is built. \isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Core}
contains the abstract crash-divergence kernel and replay/log-derived exclusion
results. \isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Execution} is the
main operational proof line: it defines the state-carrying small-step semantics,
the admissible trace wrapper, the separable non-atomic completion classes, and
the crash-closed concrete bad-crash theorem. The elevated results are carried by
eight additive theories over that line:
\isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Partition} (the two-sided
crash partition, the inherent-unsafety corollary, and the subsumption of the
structured routes),
\isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Relay} (the verified
non-atomic bounded-replay CDC relay),
\isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Converse} (the image-level
crash-safety biconditional),
\isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Premise{\isacharunderscore}Tightness}
with
\isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Refinement{\isacharunderscore}Tightness}

by a drop-fails witness, refinement in both directions),
\isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Frontier{\isacharunderscore}Seam}

\isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Image{\isacharunderscore}Completeness}

\isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Order{\isacharunderscore}Completeness}

\subsection*{What is proved}

The four headline results form a single arc; the older kernel and the additive
layers are the scaffolding they stand on. Theorem names are collected in the
result-to-theorem map below.

\paragraph{Obstruction --- the crash partition.} The safety kernel is the
absence-shaped \isa{asymmetric{\isacharunderscore}store} locale (if the
committed-log authority has advanced by a crash frontier while the downstream
still lags there, the two stores disagree there), and the operational negative
headline turns that premise-close pattern into a state-carrying execution
theorem: a crash-closed implementation admitting a separable, non-atomic
dual-write completion has a concrete crash trace with an observable mismatch.
The partition sharpens both into a biconditional over the whole domain of
crash-closed, refining implementations: reaching a reachable observable bad crash
is equivalent to reaching an \emph{exposable} scoped mismatch --- a reachable
state that disagrees with the committed-log image on a scoped key at an
in-interval frontier and is either still running or already crashed exactly
there. The two-armed disjunct makes the equivalence \emph{total}: it absorbs the
already-crashed initial-mismatch class that defeats any ``reach a running gap,
then crash'' phrasing. A follow-up shows the unsafe pole is easy to reach --- any
faithful, crash-closed implementation that performs an \emph{effective}
non-atomic source write on a monitored, in-interval key reaches the exposable
region, and hence can suffer an observable bad crash. The boundary is
effectiveness, not scope size, and the implication is one-directional.

\paragraph{Construction --- the verified relay.} The safe side is not merely a
complement; it is exhibited. A non-atomic bounded-replay relay drives its
downstream by a scope- and frontier-bounded filter of the committed source log.
The crux is that this bounded replay is source-image-equivalent to the full log
on scope at the advertised frontier, and it has no one-line proof: it is a
visibility argument over two different lists. A single advertise discipline then
does two jobs at once --- it makes ``no observable mismatch'' a \emph{discharged
inductive invariant} at every advertised frontier, and it \emph{forces} a
reachable observable bad crash in the unadvertised gap above the advertised
frontier. Recovery closes the loop by a non-atomic bounded replay that heals the
scoped image through replay, provably distinct from a wholesale atomic copy of
the source history.

\paragraph{Duality --- crash-safety is image-faithfulness.} Restricting to
running frontiers turns the partition into a clean biconditional stated purely in
terms of the store image: a crash-closed, refining implementation whose initial
state is running is crash-safe if and only if it is
\emph{running-image-faithful} --- its downstream image equals the committed
source-log image on scope at every reachable running frontier. The backward
direction rests on one elementary new ingredient, a running-predecessor lemma for
reachable crashed states. All three premises are load-bearing:
crash-closure by a genuine drop-fails witness (a running-start,
non-crash-closed, safe-yet-image-unfaithful implementation), running-start by the
already-crashed initial-mismatch class, and refinement
(witnessed in both directions by
\isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Refinement{\isacharunderscore}Tightness}:
safe-yet-unfaithful and faithful-yet-unsafe non-refining implementations).
When we call the biconditional an optimality result we mean image-tightness, not
minimality of encoding: safety forces the downstream image on scope, not the
relay's list-level delivery discipline.

\paragraph{Completeness --- crash-safety reads only the scoped image.} The
characterization is stated at the level of the scoped store image, and this is
not a modeling shortcut. Observable mismatch reads the delivered history only
through its scoped image, so any change to the delivered stream that preserves
that image --- reordering, multiplicity, extra out-of-scope events --- is
invisible to crash-safety. Concretely, neither the acknowledgement/durability
axis nor the delivery-order axis carries a second, stronger crash-safety notion:
each collapses to the image or reads a field crash-safety never touches. Within
this model, observable crash-safety is thus invariant in everything about the
delivered stream but its scoped image, with the two axes as case studies. This
is a
completeness statement \emph{for crash-safety in this execution model}, not a
claim that no richer model --- irreversible external effects above all --- could
carry more.

\paragraph{An executable decider.} For a concrete schedule the bad-crash
question is decidable by computation: the small-step semantics is deterministic
and each state field is a fold of the label list, so the run is packaged into an
executable state function proved to agree with the relational semantics, and the
bad-crash status at a given frontier and key is a total decision function proved
correct against the relational predicate and tied to divergence. On the running
example it returns true for a source write crashed before the downstream catches
up and false for the same update without the crash --- both discharged by kernel
evaluation, not an external oracle. This is deliberately a \emph{point} decider,
for a given frontier and key, which is all the partition's per-frontier statement
needs.

\paragraph{Positive replay side and additive layers.} The DBLog-free positive
side records that a downstream which is replay-derived at an advertised frontier
has no scoped mismatch there, with pure outbox/CDC segments, replay prefixes, and
certified CDC tails as constructional certificates for the replay/store-equality
premise; the older
\isa{log{\isacharunderscore}derived{\isacharunderscore}excludes{\isacharunderscore}asymmetric{\isacharunderscore}store}
theorem remains as the class-level disjointness statement. A pairwise fan-out
layer keeps one source authority and an indexed family of downstream targets, so
fan-out divergence is exactly the existence of a divergent target projection
while fan-out replay/log-derived safety requires every selected target
projection to satisfy the positive equality premise. A multi-key layer groups
keys on the same authority and proves that a crash-prefix partial materialization
can violate a stated cross-key invariant, recovering the ordinary per-key theorem
as the singleton-group case. Delivery, crash-truncation, and store-clock theories
make the pending channel, durable crash boundary, and downstream observation
clock more realistic without changing the kernel.

\paragraph{Optional DBLog companion.} A well-formed DBLog run is exhibited as one
subordinated certified inhabitant of the shared virtual-cut-state interface, in
the companion session
\isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}DBLog{\isacharunderscore}Instance}
--- the sole importer of the
\isa{DBLog{\isacharunderscore}Virtual{\isacharunderscore}Cuts} base --- closed
\emph{by citation} to that development's soundness result, not re-proved. The
event-constructor filter \isa{cdc{\isacharunderscore}only} separates the two
prongs, and a distinctness pair shows the outbox and DBLog inhabitants genuinely
differ; both refer to the same
\isa{virtual{\isacharunderscore}cut{\isacharunderscore}state} constant imported
from \isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Layer0}.

\subsection*{Scope of the claims}

Soundness means scoped-state equality at the chosen frontier: per-key in the
core theorems, and bounded source-group invariant violation in
\isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Multikey}. No general
transactional atomicity, serializability, isolation, or multidatabase atomic
commit protocol is modelled or claimed.
The obstruction is a shallow durability fact --- authority committed early,
downstream late, crash in between, hence disagreement at the crash --- and is
\emph{not} an FLP-style asynchronous-consensus impossibility. The
\isa{asymmetric{\isacharunderscore}store} premises fix value fidelity at
completion, so the divergence they force is a \emph{transient} lag across the
crash, not a permanent value mismatch. The duality biconditional and its
completeness are stated at the level of the scoped store image; the image-tightness
of the safe side does \emph{not} force the relay's list-level delivery
discipline, only its image consequence. The authority and crash frontiers remain
source coordinates; downstream-local clocks are interpreted only through the
faithful source-offset relation, not as arbitrary progress metrics. Fan-out is
pairwise: targets are compared independently with the single authority image, and
no target-to-target scheduler, all-targets atomic agreement, or 2PC/coordinator
protocol is modeled or claimed. The multi-key layer is likewise not a
global-transaction theorem: it has no autonomous database participants, votes,
prepared states, or coordinator. Every theorem is
conditional on the premises stated in its statement. The committed-log authority
\isa{log{\isacharunderscore}image} is materialized from the protocol base
\isa{pbase}, so stale updates and deletes over an existing database state are
first-class examples rather than empty-genesis special cases.

\subsection*{Trust base}

The development declares no \isa{axiomatization} and no \isa{consts}, and no
\isa{sorry}; it builds under
\isa{quick{\isacharunderscore}and{\isacharunderscore}dirty}~\isa{=}~false, and the
elevated headline theorems are additionally certified oracle-free by a dependency
probe over their proof terms. Its
  substrate is the shared DBLog-free \isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Layer0}
  parent, in which the source-coordinate type is a \isa{typedef} over the
  naturals and its order is \emph{proved} --- so nothing about the coordinate
  order is assumed. The
protocol carrier is a record; the negative and positive classes are predicates,
locales, and execution certificates over ordinary HOL definitions, all inhabited
by closed-form witnesses. The side-parametric completion class is witnessed by
both source-first examples and a downstream-first stale-update completion, and
\isa{admissible{\isacharunderscore}dw{\isacharunderscore}exec{\isacharunderscore}trace}
packages wellformed executions without adding axioms. The wrapper is not merely
a name for the raw relation:
\isa{admissible{\isacharunderscore}wrapper{\isacharunderscore}is{\isacharunderscore}nontrivial}
exhibits a wellformed-start raw trace that admissibility rejects, an
out-of-order raw source trace rejected by the same wrapper, and drop-one
negative controls for the history, pending/enqueued, and acked/source conjuncts
of \isa{wellformed{\isacharunderscore}exec{\isacharunderscore}state}. The elevated
biconditional's premises are load-bearing: crash-closure by a falsifying witness
(\isa{crash{\isacharunderscore}closed{\isacharunderscore}is{\isacharunderscore}load{\isacharunderscore}bearing})
in
\isa{Dual{\isacharunderscore}Write{\isacharunderscore}Premise{\isacharunderscore}Tightness};
refinement by falsifying witnesses in both directions
(\isa{refinement{\isacharunderscore}drop{\isacharunderscore}falsifies{\isacharunderscore}forward},
\isa{refinement{\isacharunderscore}drop{\isacharunderscore}falsifies{\isacharunderscore}forward{\isacharunderscore}exec{\isacharunderscore}type},
